#   Getting There

From experience, when proposing major changes to how things are currently done it's important to also have some sort of plan for how we get from where we are now to where we want to be.

When planning to do something there are four key things to consider...
1. What needs doing to achieve the target end-state?
2. ... what order should we do them in? 
3. ... how will we finance the things we need to do?
4. ... and, finally, how do we measure success?

Such plans do not need to be excruciatingly detailed. 
In fact the further out the plan is projected (beyond a few years) then the less detailed it should be because things change over time and new things emerge that can cause plans to change.

Even so, having a plan is not a bad idea for various reasons such as...
- It makes us think about the order that something should be done in to optimise timescales and benefits
- Some things are worth doing irrespective of what the final objectives are
- We can identify and do any essential preparatory work first
- Tasks that are independent of each other can be separated into concurrent work-flows

So what we have in this section is not a final "_it must be done exactly like this_" plan but a more flexible "_domain driven component based_" plan that has multiple potential paths to completion.

#   What Needs Doing?

Any country that already has state control or ownership of any Essential Services is already part of the way along the road to the Post Work Society.
How far along that road they are depends on which Essential Services are already controlled by Society (likely through Government control) and how closely the capabilities of those existing Essential Services match the intended target-state requirements.

So identifying what needs doing is essentially a Gap Analysis comparing "_what we have_" with "_what we want_" and filling in the gaps are the tasks that need doing. 

### The Order to do them in

In Iterative Application Development there are two main approaches to planning and executing changes once the Minimum Viable Product (MVP) has been established.
In many cases the Minimum Viable Product is whatever application already exists (if any) that provides the basic functionality that's required in the target-state application.

These are
- Function Based Development - identify some key piece of functionality and then build that specific function
- Component Based Development - pick some discrete part of the application (the Component) that has a identifiable boundary around it (its Interface) and then rebuild and improve just that Component whilst leaving the rest of the Application alone.

Most application developments are a mixture of the two


In terms of building the Post Work Society this means putting Infrastructure in place first.

##   Funding The Transition

Setting up a Post Work Society would normally cost a lot of Money to transfer critical industries into public ownership i.e. buy the outstanding shares on the open market to convert those companies into state owned enterprises.

80 years ago the solution would be easy - simply "nationalize" the industries that we need to take control over.
However, it's not as simple as that nowadays because most of the key industries are actually now owned by external parties and to simply confiscate them would cause all kinds of problems with many other countries.

Basically, we should not underestimate the political power that existing Wealth Management Corporations such as Blackrock and State Street have and their abolity to exercise that political power to retaliate against any attempt "steal their property" from them.

Even though it's a challenge to convert existing companies we can still make decisions about how we want the market to function and define constraints on future suppliers into that market.

##  Measuring Success

There's a lot of focus on Gross Domestic Product (GDP) when talking about how wealthy or successful a country or society may be but that's a really awful statistic to measure by.

The "**Happiness Index**" is a much better measure of success (though maybe not in the UK because we're a miserable bunch of people - it's due to the weather) because it takes into account not just the economic factors but also the social and environmental factors that contribute to people's overall well-being.

"**Percentage of people living a comfortable life**" is another good metric because it provides an absolute view of how well Society is meeting the needs of its members.
It doesn't matter how much wealth a country has if the majority of its population is living in poverty or struggling to make ends meet.

##  Developing A National Plan

This is where Direct Democracy becomes essential because once a National Plan is formulated then changing the National Plan should require a vote by all members of Society.
This is the only secure way of making sure that an agreed plan is followed by Government (who are the executors of the National Plan) and not subject to sudden changes in direction due to the Political Ideology of random Political Parties.

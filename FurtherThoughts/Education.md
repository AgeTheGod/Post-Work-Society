#   Continuous Education

There’s a meaningful difference between systems where people participate voluntarily, because they expect to be better off, and systems where participation is compelled.

Most kids will never be taught to think about that distinction. They’ll learn that markets exist and governments regulate them. 
They won’t learn to ask where voluntary cooperation ends and compulsion begins, or what that difference means for how people actually live.

That question applies almost everywhere. And the kids who learn to ask it early have a framework that works far beyond any economics class.

That’s what the Tuttle Twins books are built to teach. The real principles behind how systems work, introduced early and clearly enough to stick.

It’s not an either/or proposition. 
It should be a highly regulated market to promote fair competition, outlaw or regulate monopolies, make sure companies don’t externalize their costs and harm onto the public, and prevent dishonesty and dangerous products. 
The government should also provide goods and services that the private sector cannot do well such as roads, police and fire, schools, assistance for the poor and disabled, and health care. 
This is known as a Social Democracy. 
Most people in the U.S. prefer this when you remove the dishonest labels and fear mongering.

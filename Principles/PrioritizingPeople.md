#   Prioritizing People

Societies are made up of people and should be organized for the benefit of those people.

This, as far as I'm concerned, is the basis for forming any kind of Society and, as such, means that it's the needs of people in a Society have priority over the needs of any organization that might operate in that society.

As a consequence the "rights" of any kind of profit-making Commercial Organizations are a secondary consideration when it comes to deciding whether some activity is allowed or deciding whether some proposal will be beneficial to society or not.

It's a principle that we seem to have lost sight of in the last 50 years (and some countries lost sight of it before then) but is core to defining the Post Work Society and understanding the reasoning behind many of the proposals being made.

This is particularly the case when it comes to the utilization of some resource that is essential to the survival or happiness of the people living in a society.
Consequently, when there is a shortage of any given resource then the needs of the people should be met first.

How this translates into actual implemented policy depends on what is specifically being discussed.
For example, if we were talking about the cost of electricity then we might talk about differential pricing so that residential users (where people live) pay on a least cost basis whereas commercial enterprises might pay a higher commercial price to reflect the profit making motivation.

Similarly, when it comes to land usage we might decide to reserve the "best" land for populations and the most arable land for food production and place factories and warehouses in less desirable locations. 
Basically, decisions on where to locate a "bulk business" should be based on what is convenient to people and not where it is convenient for the business.
This is not as radical as it first appears once we consider "lights out" factories and warehouses are the future for many retail activities.
This would likely also have the side-effect of improving the environment of our towns and cities (which then improves happiness).

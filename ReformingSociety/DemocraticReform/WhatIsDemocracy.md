#   What Is Democracy?

"**Democracy**" is the method that the vast majority of people on the planet (including many of the so-called "Dictatorships") use to make decisions about how a society will be managed and most people that live in a Democratic Society will have an opinion on what Democracy is.
The basic definition usually includes some principle like "**One person, one vote**" 

Democracy is also a process (the "**Democratic Process**") comprised of the following basic steps...
1. Define some decision that needs to be made
2. Set a date for making that decision
3. Eligible Voters then cast their Vote on or before that date
4. Votes are counted after that date
5. A winner is decided based on the Votes cast

##  Variations of Democracy

Democracy, like most abstract complex ideas, is not a single thing and there are many, many variations in use around the world.
In practice, nearly all existing forms of Democracy take this basic "One person, one vote" principle and then introduce restrictions and constraints on various characteristics of the "**Democratic Process**".

Some of these sources capture 100's of data-points in order to classify the various Democracies being monitored. 
This produces a very fine-grained analysis but (personally speaking) loses focus on the core characteristics of the Democratic Process which are...

- How applicable is the process to the kinds of decisions that need to be made?
- How representative is the result of the overall "will of the people"?
- How open is the Democratic Process to "corruption" i.e. how easily can it be influenced by "external interests"?

##  Applicability of Democracy

In most cases Democracy tends to focus on the election of leaders but can of course be applied whenever any kind of decision needs to be made that affects the running of a Society.
For example, we might also make decisions about...
- What services will be provided by Society itself (as opposed to provision by Private Enterprise)
- How each service will be funded
- Whether the issue is a National or Local issue
- How the Democratic Process itself works and is improved over time.

It's also the case that different kinds of Democracy are better suited to to particular kinds of decision making. For example...
- "First Past The Post" is best suited to single "black or white" kinds of decisions 
- ... whereas "Proportional Representation" is better suited to decisions where there are a range of applicable options
- ... but "Preferred Option" approach is better suirted to decisions that have a range of potential solutions but only one solution can be chosen.

Hence it is clear that a "**One Size Fits All**" approach to Democracy really isn't the best approach decision making and a De3mocracy should support multiple approaches withthe best approach selected according to the type of decision being made.

This, believe it or not, seems complicated but is surprisingly simple to implement because the Democratic Process itself does not change and only the final stage "How we determine the winner" that depends on what issue is being voted on.

##  Corruptibility

This is a key characteristic that needs to be measured when appraising any Democratic System.

In this context "Corruption" is not limited to what most people would regard as "Illegal Activity". 
If we consider that all Democratic Decisions are restricted to eligible members of Society (i.e. Individual Citizens) then we also have to consider what influence Ineligible Parties (those that do not directly vote) might have over that decision making. 

Unfortunately, probably the most corruptible part of most Democratic Systems are Political Parties themselves and the sponsorship of Political Parties by Ineligible Parties. 
Most countries that allow Political Parties allow them to be actively sponsored (e.g. paid for through political donations) by just about any Person or Organization that wants to make a donation to that Political Party.
In pretty much every case that donation is in the expectation that the donor will get some "Payment In Kind" at some point in the future when the Political Party is elected to Government.

In addition, since the widespread implementation of the Internet and the global emergence of Social Media, is the emergence of "**Misinformation**" (som would call this "**out-right lying**") being fed into any political debate. 

However, these two points, although they are the most significant are not the full extent of Corruptibility and the subject is further explored in [Problems With Democracy](ProblemsWithDemocracy.md)

##  Representative Democracy

A key measure of a Democratic System is how representative the final decision is of the underlying opinions of the Electorate making the decision. 

It's this feature that leads to the main variations in the Democratic Process such as...

- "First Past The Post" is the traditional winner takes all or majority voting system where the largest number of votes cast (even if it's not a majority of votes cast) decides the winner. 
- "Proportional Representation" where votes are allocated to outcomes based on an overall percentage of votes cast for a particular preference. 
- "Preferred Option" where voters indicate select multiple choices from a set of provided options and then the lesser preferred options are eliminated from the vote count and reallocated to other options based on lower preferences. 
- "Electoral College" where voters are not voting directly for their preferred outcome. Instead they vote for a "slate" of party representatives called electors. These electors then subsequently meet after the election to officially cast the formal votes.

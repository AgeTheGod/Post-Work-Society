#   The Welfare State

Once upon a time, back in the 1940's Britain was regarded as a world-leader in Social Reforms and the instigation of the Welfare State i.e. State Run Government bodies that covered all the basic needs of it's citizens from cradle to grave.

This was famously described in the 1942 "Beveridge Report" (officially the "Social Insurance and Allied Services" report) and then enacted over the next 5 years in various bills such as...

| Act of Parliament           | Year Passed | The "Giant" Slain    | Modern Welfare Result                  |
|-----------------------------|-------------|----------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Education Act               | 1944        | Ignorance            | Free universal secondary school        |
| Family Allowances Act       | 1945        | Want / Child Poverty | Child benefit payments to mothers      |
| National Insurance Act      | 1946        | Want / Idleness      | Universal state pensions and sick pay  |
| National Health Service Act | 1946        | Disease              | Free healthcare at the point of use    |
| National Assistance Act     | 1948        | Squalor / Poverty    | Modern social services and safety nets |

However Britain was not the first country that advocated some kind of "social safety net" to meet the needs of citizens in times opf adversity (which ussually meant unemployment).
Before then we had...
- The "**Bismarkian Model**" in Germany from around 1880 which featured Health Insurance Bill (1883), Accident Insurance Bill (1884) and Old Age and Disability Insurance Bill (1889)
- The "**Universal Welfare State**" in New Zealand from 1938 and pretty much identical to the British proposal
- The "**Folkhemmet**" / People's Home in Sweden from 1932 with state-subsidised housing, universal maternity care and old-age pensions.
- The "**New Deal**" in the USA from 1935 with old-age pensions, unemployment insurance and federal aid for physically disabled children.

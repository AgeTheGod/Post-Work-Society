##  Job Guarantees

Probably seems a bit odd putting this under Social Reform rather than Economic Reform 
but given that the overall intent of a Post Work Society is to minimize the necessity of actually doing any work 
then I think Job Guarantees would be a significantly different practice.

##  The Standard Job Guarantee Approach  

The Federal Job Guarantee is a core pillar of Modern Monetary Theory. It is not just a social safety net, but a structural macroeconomic tool designed to anchor inflation and ensure full employment.

How the Program Works is as a permanent, federally funded program that is locally administered.
- Open-Ended Employment: The federal government promises to hire anyone who wants a job, regardless of skill level, background, or economic conditions.
- Federal Funding: The currency-issuing government funds 100% of the wages and benefits.
- Local Administration: Local governments, non-profits, and community groups manage the workers to ensure tasks meet real community needs (e.g., environmental cleanup, elder care, community gardens).

Modern Monetary Theory uses the Job Guarantee to solve two problems simultaneously: unemployment and inflation.
1. The Wage Anchor (Controlling Inflation) - the government sets a fixed minimum compensation package (e.g., £12.50 per hour).
   * This becomes the universal floor for the entire economy.
   * Private employers must match or beat this compensation to attract workers.
   * Because the government keeps the wage rate fixed rather than bidding against the private sector, it does not trigger a wage-price inflationary spiral. [16, 17]
2. The Economic Buffer Stock - the JG acts as an automatic shock absorber that expands and contracts with the business cycle: [18]
   * During a Recession: Private businesses lay off workers. Instead of becoming unemployed and losing income, these workers immediately enter the JG pool. Government spending automatically rises, injecting money into the economy and stabilizing demand. [19, 20, 21, 22, 23]
   * During an Economic Boom: Private employers need workers. They offer wages slightly higher than the JG base rate. Workers voluntarily leave the JG pool for private sector jobs. Government spending automatically shrinks, cooling down the economy. [24, 25, 26, 27, 28]

This is fundamentally different to traditional economic policy which uses a "buffer pool of unemployed people" to fight inflation (i.e. keeping a pool of desperate, jobless people to suppress wage growth demands). 

Modern Monetary Theory, via the Job Guarantee, replaces this with a "buffer pool of employed people" who are working for the overall benefit of society when not employed.

The result can be summarized as...

| Feature            | Traditional Unemployment Insurance    | MMT Job Guarantee                        |
|--------------------|---------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Worker Status      | Unemployed / Idle.                    | Employed / Productive.                   |
| Income Type        | Cash benefit with an expiration date. | Continuous wage for performed work.      |
| Skill Preservation | Skills erode; employment gaps widen.  | Skills are maintained or upgraded.       |
| Economic Impact    | Reduces spending power dramatically.  | Maintains a baseline of consumer demand. |

Of course there are criticisms of this approach including...
- The Efficiency Problem: Critics argue managing millions of shifting, low-skilled workers locally would create massive bureaucratic waste.
- Private Sector Crowding: Low-margin private businesses (like restaurants or retail) might go bankrupt if they cannot afford to match the government's benefit package.
- The "Make-Work" Risk: Opponents worry the program would degenerate into meaningless tasks just to keep people busy.
... but, to be honest about this, I see all those criticisms as spurious and incredibly weak arguments. (I might address them if I have to but the weakness of the criticism is quite self-evident)

In a Post-Work society, where advanced automation, artificial intelligence (AI), and robotics handle most traditional economic labor, the conceptual framework of an MMT Job Guarantee changes completely.

Instead of serving as a tool to achieve "full employment" in a corporate sense, the Job Guarantee transforms into a mechanism for social inclusion, human connection, and community care.

In a post-work world, the definition of an eligible job shifts entirely away from commercial productivity and toward tasks that AI and robots cannot, (or possibly should not) do such as...

* The Care Economy: Highly human-centric roles like companionship for the elderly, early childhood mentoring, emotional support, and grief counseling.
* Ecological Stewardship: Rewilding projects, community gardening, urban farming, and local environmental monitoring.
* Cultural and Creative Production: Local theater production, neighborhood arts initiatives, oral history archiving, and community journalism.
* Civic and Social Infrastructure: Organizing local festivals, managing neighborhood tool-sharing libraries, and coordinating community mutual aid networks.

## Job Guarantee and Universal Basic Income

The debate between MMT advocates and Universal Basic Income (UBI) advocates (and I'm a fan of both) intensifies in a post-work scenario because on the surface they appear to be conflicting (even contradictory) solutions to a similar problem . 

The MMT folks argue that even when survival is guaranteed, the Job Guarantee provides crucial psychological and social benefits that cash alone cannot replace.

| Feature                | Universal Basic Income (UBI)                                 | Post-Work Job Guarantee (JG)                                |
|------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| Primary Goal           | Economic survival and freedom from toil.                     | Social connection, agency, and community contribution.      |
| Social Structure       | Individualist; separates income from societal participation. | Collectivist; links human activity to community well-being. |
| Psychological Function | Removes poverty stress.                                      | Counters isolation, purposelessness, and social alienation. |
| Resource Allocation    | Unconditional cash distribution.                             | Voluntary participation in human-centric projects.          |

Personally I don't see this as an either/or situation where we have to choose one of the two choices and discard the other because I think both can be implemented together.

UBI is designed to give people freedom of choice

The key Mechanics of a Post-Work Job Guarantee
- Decoupling Survival from Labor : In a post-work society, basic necessities (food, housing, healthcare) are already guaranteed by the state's automated productive capacity. 
  - The JG wage becomes a discretionary i.e. it is no longer a tool to prevent starvation, but a choice to increase one's quality of life while helping the community.
- Radical Flexibility and Autonomy : Because there is no commercial urgency to produce widgets, the "jobs" are defined by the participants. If a group of citizens wants to spend 15 hours a week painting a community mural or designing an open-source video game for local youth, the government funds the project and pays them the fixed JG rate.
- The Ultimate Private Sector Competitor : If a private company still needs human workers (e.g., a high-end boutique restaurant or a boutique design firm) then its terms & conditions must compete with a government 
  - This forces remaining private employment to be exceptionally humane and highly compensated which can only be to the overall benefit of the Post Work Society

Criticisms of a Post-Work JG
- The Bureaucracy of Purpose: Critics argue that forcing people to participate in government-approved "civic activities" to earn extra money is paternalistic compared to a simple UBI.
  - This is actually garbage because there is nothing inherent in a Job Guarantee that means people *must* work. 
- The "Fake Work" Dilemma: Opponents worry that if automation handles everything efficiently, the JG would invent arbitrary, low-value tasks just to keep human beings occupied.
  - This is also a spurious argument. Most people are quite capable of keeping themselves occupied doing interesting things

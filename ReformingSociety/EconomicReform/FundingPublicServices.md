#   Funding Of Public Services

There are three basic methods to pay for anything which are...
- "**Payment On Consumption**" which typically refers to a consumption-based pricing model (also known as usage-based billing), where you pay for services or goods based on exactly how much you use, rather than a flat fee. 
- "**Payment On Account**" refers to a general partial payment made to a supplier that has not yet been matched to a specific cost, or a deposit towards future goods or service taht may be supplied.
- "**Payment Through General Taxation**"

##  General Taxation

###  Income Tax

Forms of General Taxation include...
- Wealth Tax
- Inheritance Tax
- Sales Tax
- Land & Property Tax
- Income Tax

- **Progressive Taxation** is a tax system where the tax rate increases as the taxable amount increases. This means that the wealthy pay a higher percentage of their income in taxes than the poor

Normally this is applied to Income Tax but it could also be applied to other forms of Taxation as well.

It's also worth considering whether there should be separate Tax Regimes depending on whether the Taxable Legal Entity is a Person or a Corporation, and whether the Taxable Legal Entity is a Resident or a Non-Resident or wholly External Entity.

Basically a Society...
- Should discourage people to hoard wealth, because it is not good for society as a whole.
- Should give preferential treatment to its own members over non-members, because it is not good for a society to be taken advantage of by outsiders.
- Should have a Taxation system that is progressive and that is designed to ensure that the wealthy are paying their fair share of taxes and that is designed to discourage hoarding wealth.

###  Gradual redistribution of Wealth

The main problem with Wealth in the UK is that it's not evenly distributed and a very, very small percentage of the population hoards the vast majority of ther wealth.
This isn't something that has happened accidentally and in most cases the wealth has been accumulated over many generations.

Hoarding wealth is a common practice among the rich, but it can have negative consequences for society as a whole. When the wealthy hoard their wealth, they are not investing it in the economy or using it to create jobs.
This can lead to economic stagnation and increased inequality.

We can even go so far as argue that hoarding wealth is theft from the rest of society, because the wealthy are taking resources that could be used to improve the lives of everyone and instead are using it to enrich themselves.

Unfortunately for many reasons, we can't just take the Wealth away from people just because we want to.

Given that building a Post-Work Society will take a generation (at least) to complete and that creating the proposed services will need funding during the transition period in order to buy some of the resources required (no geographic area has everything that's needed) then it seems reasonable that we would only want to raise the necessary capital gradually as well.  

There are two kinds of Progressive Tax mechanisms that would allow us to do this...
- A Wealth Tax on the accumulation of long-term assets
- Inheritance Tax on the transfer of assets on the death of the current owner.

###  Removing the "Tax Loopholes"

The UK (along with most other countries in the world, has far too many Tax Loopholes designed explicitly to allow the Wealthy to keep a disproportionate percentage of the countries resources for themselves.
We have...
- Non-Dom Tax Exemptions
- Residency in Off-Shore Tax Havens
- ISA's
- Private Pensions
- Trust Funds

##  Ring Fenced Funding

Ring-fencing in finance is a regulatory requirement, notably in the UK since January 2019, that forces large banks to legally separate their core retail banking services (deposits, checking) from riskier investment banking activities. 
It acts as a protective "fence" to ensure essential customer services are not compromised by financial shocks elsewhere in the bank group.

I worked on Client Cash Segregation application back in the early 2010's before the "Ring Fenced Funding" regulations were passed into law amd this made sop much sense.

It also has other applications in the area of infrastructure funding through taxation (which itself is not an entirely new concept) because I think most people would be relatively happier to pay theit taxes if they knew exactly what those taxes were going to be spent on.


#   Social Economics

In his book [Vampire Capitalism: Fractured Societies and Alternative Futures](https://books.google.com/books/about/Vampire_Capitalism.html?id=HI_LDQAAQBAJ), sociologist Paul Kennedy argues that to fix the system, society must transition toward "alternative social economies" that exist outside of traditional corporate control.
Kennedy suggests that modern society's obsession with individual success and consumerism isolates people, draining away their power to make real changes. To counter this, he proposes building cooperative networks focused on community survival, sustainability, and human well-being rather than endless profit.

## 1. Shifting from Individualism to Collective Action

* The Problem: Modern consumer culture forces people to focus entirely on personal branding, individual self-realisation, and taking on personal debt. This leaves citizens socially fragmented and unable to work together. 
* The Fix: Communities must intentionally rebuild social ties to unlock their capacity for collective action. Only organized communities have the leverage to confront massive threats like climate change and corporate exploitation.

## 2. Developing Alternative Social Economies

* The Problem: The current global market is disengaged from local human needs, choosing instead to focus on "financialisation" (making money purely through trading abstract financial assets like debt and stocks).
* The Fix: Society should create and protect alternative spaces that operate outside of purely capitalist logic. Kennedy highlights case studies from around the world, pointing toward economic spaces such as:
* Worker cooperatives where employees democratically own and share the business's profits.
    * Ethical and green businesses that prioritize local sustainability over shareholder value.
    * Community-sharing networks that swap tools, food, housing, and labor directly without going through corporate middlemen.

## 3. Re-linking Technology to Public Need

* The Problem: Rapid technological advancements are currently used by corporations to cut labor costs, increase worker surveillance, and maximize executive payouts.
* The Fix: Technology should be redirected to serve the public good. Automation and digital tools should be used to reduce the burden of work on citizens and solve environmental problems, rather than simply funnelling more wealth to a "tech elite".

## 4. Overthrowing False Divisions

* The Problem: Corporate and political systems frequently distract citizens from economic exploitation by fueling artificial cultural wars. Dividing people by race, gender, generation, and class stops them from uniting.
* The Fix: Kennedy advocates for a "cosmopolitan" mindset where workers and citizens see past these manufactured differences. Recognizing shared human goals allows the public to reclaim a "sober sense of reality" and challenge the economic elite together.

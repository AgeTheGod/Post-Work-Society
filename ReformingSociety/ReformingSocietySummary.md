# Reforming Society Summary

This is where we get to the important bit. 

We knows what the trends are that need to be addressed and we have some idea of the technology available either now or in the near future that will help solve these problems and we kow what the essential goods & services are that we want to deliver. 
So now we need to put together some proposals for how we construct a sustainable Post-Work Society. 

Obviously this is a complicated subject and, consequently, "reform" is not a one dimensional thing with a single solution that will solve all our problems.

This is where I think a lot of the ideas being pushed by one group or another currently fall down. 
An economic theory such as [Guaranteed Minimum Income](./EconomicReform/GuaranteedMinimumIncome) or [State Owned Enterprises](./EconomicReform/StateOwnedEnterprises) or any of the other proposed economic reforms are not going to solve problems on their own and even in combination won't work without also [Reforming Politics](./DemocraticReform/DemocraticReformSummary) to extend democracy to more people and reduce the influence of "powerful people" and [Social Reform](./SocialReform/SocialReformSummary) to change the way people view their place in society.

All three of these aspects need to be changed concurrently and it's a bit of a rats nest of interrelated topics but in line with my fondness for Domain Decomposition as an approach to solving difficult problems I tried to break it down into manageable chunks and then look at each one in turn.
So I've broken it down into...
- [Democratic Reform](DemocraticReform/DemocraticReformSummary)
- [Economic Reform](EconomicReform/EconomicReformSummary)
- [Social Reform](SocialReform/SocialReformSummary)

##  Dealing With Blockers to Progress

Whilst pondering potential solutions a key activity is identifying the "**Blockers to Progress**" and especially the "**Show-Stoppers**" (it's a standard part of any project initiation process).
We can then decide what we CAN DO and, in some cases, MUST DO to mitigate those Blockers to Progress.

Unfortunately most of those blockers are not technical blockers but social blockers inherent in the way that we have currently structured.

Many of the blockers to progress are often deeply ingrained in the way that we do things (or how we are taught that it works) and privileges are often protected by powerful vested interests that will try to stop any change from happening.

It's also worth noting that the single biggest blocker to progress, which cuts across all of the above, is that we have a tendency to focus on short-term gains rather than long-term benefits.
This  makes it difficult to implement policies that would allow us to transition to a post-work society because the transition itself is a multi-year (possibly multi-decade) activity that will cause a certain amount of pain to different parts of society as it progresses.

Tis last point, on its own, will cause a lot of resistance from some sections of society and the reason why I'm suggesting that we actually need to [National Plan](../GettingThere/NationalPlan) to ensure that we know where we're going and how we'll get there eventually.

#   Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a branch of computer science that builds software and systems capable of performing tasks that typically require human intelligence. Instead of relying strictly on rigid, pre-written rules, AI systems use complex mathematics and data to learn, reason, solve problems, and adapt over time.

At the moment AI is an emerging technology (less than 10 years old) and very much in the "_Peak of Inflated Expectations_" subject to stage of the Garner Hype-cycle. 
As such it's still very much debatable where this technology will ultimately end up and what tasks it will be best suited for.

However, it's an evolution in technology and its precursors are much more established than that.
I was working on Natural Language Processing in the early 2000's and Expert Systems using rule-sets defined in natural language in the 1990's. 
So that's not exactly new when it comes to Information Technology! 

The ultimate, long-term goal that gets mentioned a lot is **Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)** a theoretical software that can think, learn, and apply knowledge across any subject just like a human being. 
This is still theoretical and _nobody is currently anywhere near being able to achieve Artificial General Intelligence_ so this is not part of the technology beingh considered.

Ultimately, AI like every other kind of software running on a computer, serves to...
- automate repetitive work,
- process massive amounts of data instantly, and
- assist humans in making faster, more informed decisions.

##  Why This Matters

Because AI like every other kind of software, is simply algorithms (albeit comnplex algorithms) running on a computer it has the potential to be very flexible and hence useful.

With AI the flexibility is defined by the data-sets that are used to create the Large or Small Large Models that are then used

Basically, with a suitable quality data-set an general purpose AI can be taught to perform any definable task that falls within its area of expertise.

##  Types of AI

Every AI tool currently in existence is classified as Narrow AI, meaning it is highly skilled at a single, specific job (hence the sub-classification above) but lacks true human understanding (which means it is unable to differentiate between reasonable and unreasonable statements given to it as input).

Today, AI is generally categorised into the following specific functional types (though more functions types are identified regularly)...
- **Analytical AI** scans massive data-sets to find patterns and predict trends, acting as a digital detective.
- **Generative AI** acts as a creator, producing original text, artwork, images, and code from simple user prompts.
- **Agentic AI**, which powers digital assistants and customer chatbots tht perform some process or produces some outcome based on triggering events
- **Visual AI**, which lets machines interpret videos or photos, and

Of these the two that I'm mostly interested in are Analytical AI to make decisions and Agentic AI to perform some action based on those decisions.

### Analytical AI

**Analytical AI** scans massive data-sets to find patterns and predict trends, acting as a digital detective.

Predictive Data Analytics itself is not a new science - when I was at school we used to call this Statistics and people have been producing statistics for centuries with pen & paper.


* Data Processing: It sweeps through huge datasets much faster than any human team could.
* Pattern Recognition: It links random data points together to see subtle correlations.
* Continuous Learning: Every time new information arrives, the AI updates its logic to make its future guesses even more accurate. [1, 2, 8, 9, 10]

This matters because traditional data analysis applications (and I've built many of these) required input data to be thoroughly cleaned are rigorously structured, usually as "relational data", in order to be usable in traditional data analysis applications.  

Analytical AI automates the boring preparation work, handles messy data (like scattered text documents or images), and provides fast, near real-time guidance so businesses can make smart decisions quickly.

###  Agentic AI

Agentic AI refers to advanced artificial intelligence systems that can act autonomously, make independent decisions, and complete multi-step tasks with minimal human intervention. Unlike traditional or standard generative AI models that passively wait for a user prompt to generate a single response, agentic AI is proactive and goal-driven. You provide the system with a high-level objective, and it figures out how to achieve it on its own. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]

An agentic system typically operates in a continuous loop—often called a perception-action loop—defined by four main capabilities...
- Autonomy: It completes complex tasks from start to finish without needing step-by-step human prompts or constant oversight.
- Reasoning and Planning: It takes a massive goal, breaks it down into smaller, logical sub-tasks, and designs its own execution pathway.
- Tool Usage: It can actively interact with the digital or physical world by calling APIs, browsing the web, querying databases, running code, or modifying software systems.
- Reflection and Adaptation: If the AI encounters a roadblock or an error, it evaluates what went wrong, self-corrects, and tries a different approach.

Note on Terminology: People often confuse AI Agents with Agentic AI. 
Think of an AI Agent as an individual worker or a building block trained for a specific task (e.g., a code writer or a data retriever). Agentic AI is the overarching framework or "mesh" where multiple agents, tools, and databases are orchestrated together to run massive, complex business operations.

The key difference between Agentic AI and and more traditional Business Process Management (BPM) applications is that BPM is usually an explicitly procedural activity whose steps are hard-coded into the process.

Agentic AI, on the other hand, infers the process (the Reasoning and Planning stage) from the environment in which its operating and may change the process when the environment changes e.g. new services and API's are made available.

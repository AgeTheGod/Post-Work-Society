
#   Water

### Desalination

Four hundred million people. That is more than the entire population of the United States.
China just solved its water scarcity problem for all of them. They did not legislate water rations or wait around for a drought crisis. They simply built the largest solar-powered desalination network in human history along their eastern seaboard.
The system pulls the ocean in. It uses massive coastal solar farms to strip the salt away. It pumps clean drinking water inland at a scale never before attempted. No rivers are drained. The system pulls the ocean in. It uses massive coastal solar farms to strip the salt away. It pumps clean drinking water inland at a scale never before attempted. No rivers are drained. No underground aquifers are depleted. They essentially turned the ocean into an infinite reservoir, powered entirely by sunlight.

The historical nightmare of desalination has always been the power bill. Pushing ocean water through reverse osmosis membranes requires colossal amounts of energy, which traditionally meant burning immense quantities of fossil fuels just to get a glass of clean water.
By hardwiring these massive desalination plants directly into dedicated coastal solar infrastructure, they bypassed the core flaw of the technology. The energy is free and carbon-neutral.
The next global engineering hurdle is the brine—the hyper-salty byproduct of the process. But even that is shifting from a waste problem to a resource, with modern facilities increasingly extracting commercially vital minerals like lithium and magnesium from the discharge before it is diluted and released.

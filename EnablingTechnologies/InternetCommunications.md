#   Internet Communications

The **Internet** allows us to communicate and access information in ways that were not possible before. This is a key enabler in many areas such as...
- **Education** allows us to access educational resources and to learn about things that are of interest to us without having to go to a physical location.
- **Access Public Services**
- **E-Commerce** allows us to buy and sell goods and services online without having to go to a physical store.


Technically this is already here and available to most people in the UK (and many other parts of the world) but most people are completely unaware of it.





What we have to do to do this is...
- Build a **Nationwide Communication Back-Bone**
- Provide free (no subscription) access to the Internet
- Supply an entry level Tablet PC to all Adults

...and there's so much free-to-use (mostly open source) software available to do all kinds of tasks that there is pretty much no need for anyone to ever need to buy any software from any commercial organization.

##  Build a Nationwide Communication Back-Bone

Luckily enough we probably don;t have tro do this because a Nationwide Communication Back-Bone already exists in the UK. It's OpenReach from BT which operates the vast majority of the communication cables in the UK.

So we could Buy OpenReach from BT which would cost approximately £15 Billion to buy outright based on...
- BT's total market value is around £24 Billion pounds ()as at 11/May-2026)
- OpenReach contributes less than 50% to BT's EBITA
- It would require a premium on top of that to keep the BT Shareholders happy (though don;t have to because the Government could just enforce it)

With the back-bone in place and the cost spread over a 10 year period (not long in terms of infrastructure operations) then the cost would be in the region of £21.50 per year or £1.80 per month per person. 

    Cost = 15,000,000,000
    Population = 70,000,000
    Amortization Period = 10 Years 
so

    15,000,000,000 / ( 70,000,000 * 10 ) = 21.4286     

Add something on top to cover future capital costs for upgrades and maintenance and we'd still only be looking at around £40 per person per year. 

That's not much at all really.

On the other hand, because it's in the national interest, the UK Government could just nationalize OpenReach because it's critical infrastructure. 
The only reason I'm avoiding that suggestion as part of the proposal is because "nationalization" doesn't go down to well in some other countries and we don't want to alienate potential external investors in other areas. 

##  Provide free (no subscription) access

Given the size of the up-front capital investment for the internet back-bone it's tempting to make this a paid for service but given how critical Internet access is to everything else that I'm proposing I think this has to be a "**Free at the point of use**" service (as per the NHS) 

However, this doesn't mean that we can't charge profit-making enterprises a fee for accessing it. 
Commercial Organizations should pay for access because their intent is to make money from that access but Non-Profits or Public Service Organizations obviously don't. 
I'm ignoring this for the moment.

##  Supply an entry level Tablet PC to all Adults

    How much to supply an entry level Tablet PC to all Adults in the UK?

Based on projected 2026 statistics, supplying an entry-level tablet to all adults in the UK would cost approximately £5.5 billion to £8.3 billion in hardware costs.
Here is the breakdown based on 2026 projections:
1. Target Audience (UK Adult Population)Total Adults (2026): Estimated ~55.65 million.
2. Cost Per Unit (Entry-Level Tablet, 2026)
   2. Low-End Retail/Bulk: Based on entry-level models like the Amazon Fire HD 8, Lenovo Tab One, or Samsung Galaxy Tab A9, bulk/wholesale prices could potentially be negotiated to around £100 per unit.
   3. Slightly Higher Entry-Level: For slightly more robust 10-inch models, prices are around £150 per unit.
3. Total Estimated Cost (Hardware Only)
   3. Budget Scenario (£100/unit): (55,653,768 * £100 ) = £5.57 Billion
   4. Moderate Scenario (£150/unit): (55,653,768 * £150 ) = £8.35 Billion
 
Key Considerations
-    Wholesale Pricing: Bulk purchasing for the entire adult population would significantly reduce retail prices, but these figures do not include VAT.
-    Hidden Costs: The total project cost would be significantly higher when including distribution logistics & IT support
-    Device Lifespan: Entry-level tablets often have limited software support, likely requiring replacement within 3–4 years.

That would be the estimate if nobody already had equipment that could access the Internet but luckily that is not the case in the UK. 
So initially we'd only need to cover the cost of supplying equipment to people that did not already have it. 
Estimates for that range between 1.5% and 4% of the adult population so around £100M to £200M for full coverage.

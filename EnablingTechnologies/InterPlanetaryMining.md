#   Interplanetary Mining

This particular subject has fascinated me ever since I first read about it in Arthur C. Clarke's "_Profiles of the Future_" and Asteroid Mining has long been a trope in most "near space" science fiction for the last 100+ years.
However, it's no longer Science Fiction because it's now a real thing that is being actively researched and developed by multiple organizations around the world.

## Resource Surveying

This has actually been ging on for over 20 years now with various space agencies and private companies using sending probes to survey the resources available in near Earth orbit and the Asteroid Belt.

The numbers here are staggering and the potential benefits to humanity are enormous.
For example, the total tonnage of "industrial gold" is estimated to be around 32,000 Tonnes worldwide (with another 200,000 Tonnes used for Jewellery and Gold Bullion reserves).
However in near Earth orbit alone there are estimated to be around 100,000 Tonnes of "industrial gold" and in the Asteroid Belt there are estimated to be around 1,000,000,000 Tonnes of "industrial gold".

Other precious metals such as Platinum and Palladium are also estimated to be present in similar quantities in near Earth orbit and the Asteroid Belt.

So, OK it would crash the Commodity price of Gold (and any other Commodity that can be mined in space) to make it pretty much worthless but that's OK because the entire intent of crteatring the Post Work Society is to create a society where money is not the measure of wealth and the cost of production has been removed or minimised as much as possible.

##  Propulsion Systems

### Plasma Propulsion Engines

Every rocket ever launched shares the same basic flaw that, in order to go further you need more fuel but more fuel makes the rocket heavier, which means you need even more fuel just to lift it. 
It is a mathematical trap that has limited space exploration for decades. 

French engineers have figured out how to bypass it and built an engine that runs entirely on electromagnetic plasma without a single moving part to break down in the vacuum of deep space. 
It accelerates ionized plasma through magnetic fields, requiring only electricity to produce continuous thrust.

The implications of this should be obvious because deep space probes no longer have a hard distance limit based on the size of their fuel tanks. 

So, whilst they don't produce the explosive force needed to break Earth's gravity from the launchpad, once they are already in orbit, their continuous, gentle acceleration makes them the fastest and most efficient way to travel through the solar system.

To clarify the "no fuel" aspect: while traditional chemical rockets require massive, heavy tanks of liquid hydrogen and oxygen to create explosions, plasma engines rely on electrical power (usually gathered via solar panels) to ionize trace amounts of gas. 

Because they accelerate particles using magnetism rather than fire, they are incredibly efficient so a plasma system the size of a suitcase can do the interplanetary work of a chemical booster the size of a bus.

#   Keep It Simple, Stupid (KISS)

"_Perfection is achieved, not when there is nothing more to add, but when there is nothing left to take away_" : Antoine de Saint-Exupéry

While he is most famous globally for authoring the beloved children's book The Little Prince, he actually wrote this specific line in his 1939 memoir, Terre des Hommes (published in English as "_Wind, Sand and Stars_").

He was describing the functional evolution of airplane design and that as machine engineering advanced, planes became less bulky and complicated. 
The clunky, exposed mechanical additions of early aviation gradually disappeared, leaving behind a beautifully streamlined, efficient, and simple fuselage. 
To him, a machine was perfect only when it had been stripped down to its absolute, pure essence - the "**Minimalist Design**" principle.

"Keep It Simple, Stupid" (KISS) is the modern equivalent of that design philosophy arguing that most systems function optimally if they are kept simple rather than made complex. 
It serves as a strict warning against over-engineering, unnecessary sophistication, and clutter, emphasizing that simplicity should be the primary goal in any proposed solution.

The goal of the KISS principle is not to create a cheap, incomplete solution, but rather to find the most direct path to solve a problem without adding unnecessary baggage.

This is why I've coupled it with the Antoine de Saint-Exupéry "Perfection" quote because whilst the rule is a highly desirable design objective it also requires finding a balance to avoid over-simplification...

| The Benefits of KISS                                                                                         | The Danger of Over-Simplification                                                                                        |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Fewer Points of Failure: Less parts or lines of code mean fewer opportunities for things to break.           | Loss of Crucial Nuance: Over-simplifying complex topics (like medicine, law, or economics) can result in misinformation. |
| Faster Upkeep: Simple setups allow anyone to step in and troubleshoot immediately.                           | Lack of Necessary Features: Stripping down a tool too much might leave it unable to solve the actual problem.            |
| Better Adaptability: It is much easier to scale up a simple foundation than it is to untangle a complex web. | Reduced Security: Omitting vital validation steps in software to keep it "clean" creates massive security loopholes.     |

```text
    I'll go as far as saying that KISS should also apply to written work such as this one.
    Instead of relying on dense jargon and wordy explanations to sound impressive, effective communicators structure their points to be short, sharp, and easy to retain.
    I'm not sure I'll achieve that objective but still something to bear in mind.
```

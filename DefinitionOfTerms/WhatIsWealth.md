#   What Is Wealth?

In its simplest sense "**Wealth**" is the net value of accumulated assets minus any outstanding liabilities such as Debt.

Even then it can be categorized in multiple ways.
Financial institutions and economists classify wealth into distinct categories based on its tangibility, liquidity, origin, and legal structure.

| Classification                  | Definition                                                                                                                                                                      |
|---------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Net Worth                       | Calculated as total assets minus total liabilities. This is the standard metric for true financial health e.g. "_High Net Worth Individual_"                                    |
| Gross Wealth                    | Total value of everything an individual owns ignoring any outstanding debts or liabilities                                                                                      |
| Physical Wealth                 | Concrete, tangible assets such as real estate, gold, vehicles, and art that holds intrinsic or market value                                                                     |
| Financial (Liquid) Wealth       | Cash or easily convertible assets such as bank deposits and money market funds                                                                                                  |
| Notional (Unrealised) Wealth    | Paper wealth based on current market valuation such as unsold stocks & shares, crypto, or private equity.                                                                       |
| Generational (Inherited) Wealth | Wealth accumulated by previous generations and passed down through families via trusts or wills                                                                                 |
| Self-Made Wealth                | Wealth accumulated within a single individual's lifetime through theirt won economic activity                                                                                   |
| Investable Wealth               | Used by Financial Institutions to bracket Wealth based on **liquid investable assets**, excluding personal properties like a primary home, vehicles, pensions, or collectibles. |

In addition, when we talk about the Asset Value we have multiple ways of calculating that depending on the Asset Type.
The chosen method depends entirely on whether the asset is Income Generating, has a Market Quoted Price or is a unique Collectible item.
These are just some of the methods but not an exhaustyive list of Asset Types.

| Asset Type            | Valuation Method  | Approach                                                                                                                         | 
|:----------------------|-------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Real Estate           | Market value      | Compares the price per square metre or square foot of recently sold neighboring properties.                                      |
| Rental Real Estate    | Future Income     | Values an asset based on the future cash it will generate and converts future earnings into today's money using a discount rate. | 
| Public Company Shares | Market Price      | Values an asset based on recent sales of similar assets in an open, active markets                                               |
| Machinery             | Replacement Cost  | Values an asset based on what it would cost to build or replace it from scratch minus "wear & tear" depreciation.                | 
| Holding Companies     | Net Asset Value   | Totaling all physical assets and subtracting liabilities                                                                         |
| Collectibles          | Fair Market Value | Original Cost multiplied by an Asset Appreciation factor calculated from sales of similar items.                                 |

The distinction between these various Classification, Asset Types and Valuation Methods becomes very important when looking at topics such as Wealth Distribution (and Redistribution) and Progressive Taxation.

Values an asset based on the future cash it will generate and converts future earnings into today's money using a discount rate.

The buckets themselves are not mutually exclusive and which bucket a given asset falls into may not always be obvious.
For example, if a Company Share is held in a Market Quoted Corporation then it is easily fungible and can be sold on whichever Market lists it anytime that Market is open.
However, if the Company Share is in a Private Company then another private buyer needs to be located before that Company Share can be sold which makes the Company Share much less fungible and the Market Value of the Company Share (what it will sell for) more difficult to identify.

These differences are what makes Wealth difficult to calculate (or even define).

#   Factors of Production

"**Factors of Production**" is a term used in modern Economics to describe the complete list of inputs required to generate an output. 

The Factors of Production are categorized into distinct co-equal groups with the distinction primarily defined by the means by which that group is reward for their contribution. 

| Resource Input    | Purpose                                                                                                                                       |
|-------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Natural Resources | All naturally occurring resources whose supply was inherently provided by nature including Plants and Animals as well as inanimate resources. |
| Labour            | The physical and mental exertion of human beings used to create goods or services.                                                            |
| Tools             | Any previously created man-made artefacts (including Money) used by Labour to convert Natural Resources into more Man-Made Artefacts          |
| Motivation        | The people who identify a demand for some product or service and organize an enterprise to meet that demand.                                  |

All together these factors are known as the "**Factors of Production**" and in Classical Economics the Labour, Resources (Land), and Capital factors were defined not just as physical inputs, but as social classes that determined how wealth was distributed across society.

On that basis it's the relationship between these four groups, and who should get what benefit from participation, that underpins pretty much all discussions about the Economics of Production. 

##  Definition of Terms

In the above table I've only provided general definition of terms for each of the Factors of Employment. 

This is deliberate because the exact definition of each term depends on the context in which the term is being used, the particular type of economics being discussed and the scope of the Enterprise being operated.

For example, at the micro-scale a single worker might buy their tools from a tool-maker. The tool is then Capital but if the Worker made the tool themselves then it becomes a Product they produced as an output from their Enterprise i.e. a Product created from Resources and Labour

However, at the macro scale we could be discussing a nation state (or even the entire world) in which case anything that is man-made internal to that Nation State is no longer Capital but the product of Resource and Labour. 
This was the key point that Adam Smith was making in "Wealth of Nations" where he considered Capital for be any man-made resource that needed to be purchase or sourced from some supplier external to the Nation State tat the Nation State then used as input to produce some other product.

As a consequence, because we have this situation where the same thging could be classified differently according the context in which it is being discussed there is little choice than to provide generic definitions for generic terms and then discuss exact definitions case by case when they arise.  
(Unfortunately after decades working as an Information Architecxt I learnt the hard way that exact definitions are difficult tpo provide without context. This is the exact premise of Noam Chomsky's "_Meaning of Meaning_" work.)

## The Evolution of the Factors Of Production

Although Factors Of Production has been a recognized grouping concept for over 400 years the individual Factors of Production were not all defined at the same time. 
Instead Factors Of Production evolved over time as new constituent parts have been identified and defined. The timeline for that evolution is...

_Sir William Petty_, in his "Treatise of Taxes and Contributions" (1662), originally laid out a two-factor theory and famously declared that "_Labour is the father and active principle of wealth, as lands are the mother._"

_Adam Smith_, in The Wealth of Nations (1776), identified three corresponding categories of income: wages, rent, and profit. 
He explained that these incomes go to three different classes of people based on what they contribute to production, but he did not strictly group them under the precise modern definitions of production inputs.

_Jean-Baptiste Say_, in "A Treatise on Political Economy" (1803), separated these inputs into three scientific agents of production: Human Industry (Labour), Nature (Land/Resources), and Capital. 
Say argued that all three must work together to create a finished product.

_John Stuart Mill_, in his "Principles of Political Economy" (1848), solidified Say’s formulation into mainstream English economics where he explicitly stated that the "requisites of production" are land, labour, and capital.

_Alfred Marshall_, in his "Principles of Economics" (1890), was the first to formally propose and establish "Organisation" (Entrepreneurship/Enterprise) as the fourth factor of production.
Marshall expanded the traditional classical list (Land, Labour, Capital) by explicitly adding a fourth element. 
He argued that natural resources, physical tools, and raw human effort are useless unless a dynamic coordinator organizes them into a productive business structure.

The final work listed above is widely accepted as being the point where Economics shifted from Classical Economics to Neoclassical Economics.

##  "Factors of Production" vs "Means of Production" 

"Means of Production" is a commonly used by many people as an alternative to "Factors of Production" but they actually mean to different concepts.

Both terms have existed side-by-side since the 19th century, serving entirely different functions depending on the economic school of thought.
The shift that did happen was institutional: as mainstream academic economics transitioned from Marxist/Classical Economics to Neoclassical Economics between the 1870s and 1920s, "factors of production" became the dominant phrase taught in textbooks, while "means of production" became a phrase primarily used in political and social philosophy.
The core difference between the two terms explains why they exist simultaneously.

| Concept               | What It Actually Means                                                                                                                             | Who Uses It                         |
|-----------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Factors of Production | The complete list of inputs required to generate an output. This includes human labor as a co-equal ingredient alongside physical tools.           | Mainstream / Neoclassical Economics |
| Means of Production   | The physical, inanimate things required for work. This includes land, buildings, raw materials, and machinery. It explicitly excludes human labor. | Classical & Socialist Economics     |

In simple terms, a factory building and a robotic arm are both means of production and factors of production. 
However, the human worker operating the robot is only a Factor of Production. They cannot be a Means of Production unless they are literally enslaved.

In early Classical Economics works there was no clear distinction and, in fact, Classical Economists like Adam Smith and Jean-Baptiste Say used various phrasing, including "agents of production", "component parts", and "factors", interchangeably. 

Karl Marx actually used both terms in his writings. In Capital (1867), he defined "_Factors of Production_" as a broad combination of the subjective factor (human labor power) and the objective factors (the physical materials). 
He reserved means of production strictly for the physical elements.

It subsequently became even more confusing when sopme political thinkers argued that Labour was the only Means of Production becaue it's human effort that converts Natural Resources into finished Goods. This, of course, is the opposite of the definition used in Economics. 

Subsequently, the linguistic divide became absolute during the 20th century and nowadays the usage of the two terms:
* "Factors of Production" is primarily used in Economics as a neutral, depoliticized way to teach how business management works under capitalism.
* "Means of Production" became a heavily charged political term. Because Marxists argued over who should own the physical factories (the state vs. private capitalists), the phrase became synonymous with debates on communism, socialism, and class struggle.

It's because of this final point that I'm primarily using the term "Factors of Production" rather than "Means of Production" but if I do use "Means of Production" then I mean it in the political sense of involuntary labour.

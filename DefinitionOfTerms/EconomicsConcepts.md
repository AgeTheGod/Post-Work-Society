##  Economics Concepts

##  Economics

Economics is the study of how people, businesses, and governments make choices when resources are limited.
While an Economy is the actual system where buying and selling happens, Economics is the study of how that system works.

The driving force behind all of economics managing Scarcity.

- The Problem: The world has a limited amount of resources (oil, gold, time, labour).
- The Reality: Humans have an unlimited desire for goods, services, comfort, and safety.
- The Result: Because we cannot have everything we want, we must make choices. Every choice involves a trade-off.

So, in essence **Economics is the study of trade-offs!** and, at its core, Economics is a social science that tries to understand how the world allocates its scarce resources to satisfy human needs and endless wants.

To understand economics, you only need to know a few foundational ideas:

- Supply and Demand: This is the invisible force that sets prices. If a lot of people want an item (high demand) but there are very few available (low supply), the price goes up.
- Opportunity Cost: This is the value of the next best option you give up when you make a choice. If you spend £10 on a cinema ticket, the opportunity cost is the book you could have bought with that same £10.
- Incentives: These are rewards or punishments that motivate people to act in a certain way. For example, a sale (short term price cut) at a shop motivates people to buy more, while a tax on sugary drinks motivates people to buy less.

Economics is generally split into two halves, looking at the world through either a magnifying glass or a telescope.
- Micro-Economics studies small, individual units like a person, household, or a specific enterprise
- Macro-Economics studies the big picture such as entire countries or global systems.

### Why Does Economics Matter?

Economics is not just about money, Wall Street, or stock charts. It helps us understand human behavior (hence why it's regarded as a Social Science) and solve massive global issues, such as:
- How to reduce poverty and unemployment.
- How to prevent environmental damage while still growing industries.
- How governments should spend tax money on public health and education.

Economics, in addition to dealing with the questions of how to organize and manage an Economy, also provides the tools to measure if an economy is healthy and thriving, or weak and struggling.
This is done via various metrics such as...
- Gross Domestic Product (GDP): The total value of all goods and services made in a country over a specific time period.
- Employment Rates: The number of people who have jobs versus those who are actively looking for work.
- Inflation: The rate at which the prices of everyday items increase over time.

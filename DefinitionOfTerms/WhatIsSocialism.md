#   What Is Socialism?

Note: lots of people will read this and claim that this is not Socialism and instead quote some ultra-hardline definition usually from some text written 100+ years ago (Lenin is popular) when the world was very different.
However, I don't care about those very old definitions because Socialism, like Capitalism, has evolved over time and this is my current definition of what a Socialist Enterprise would be. 
It may itself change as we progress through the implementation of a Post Work Society.

Socialism is a social and economic theory that advocates for the collective ownership and control of the means of production and distribution of goods and services. 
The idea is that by having the state control these critical services, it can ensure that they are provided to everyone in society, regardless of their ability to pay.

However, (and this is an important point), Socialism does not mandate that all means of production must be provided by the state and private ownership is illegal (I believe that's Communism which is a separate Economic Theory) but does mandate that all critical services essential to the well-being of Society should be controlled by the state. 

"_Controlled by the State_" means that either the Enterprise itself is owned and operated by the Government or that the market in which the Enterprise operates is highly regulated by the Government (usually via a Closed Market). 

Beyond those critical services people are then free to produce any "luxury" goods or provide any "community services" that they wish.

That's pretty much the case across the entire planet and really the arguments come down to how much should be provide by the state as "essential services" and how much is left to others, both individuals and corporations, to provide as "value add services"?

That final question is what distinguishes the many variants of Socialism from each other. 
Like the discussion on "What id Capitalsim" the answer is not universally agreed but one possible answer, what I think is the minimum required, is covered in [Providing Essential Services](../ProvidingEssentialServices/ProvidingEssentialServicesSummary)

In addition, the motivation from providing a Service under Socialism is much different to Capitalism.

The economic motivation, i.e. the desired outcome that causes the economic activity to occur, in Capitalism is "Profit" whereas the economic motivation in Socialism is "Social Benefit".
In all other respects the Factors of Production remain the same.

Consequently, we can define an Enterprise as being a Socialist Enterprise if its...
- ultimate beneficial owner is Society as a whole rather than some sub-set of the citizens (or possibly even non-citizens)
- intent is to provide an essential service to all members of that Society at minimal cost or "not for profit".
- production targets are set by Society rather than by the Enterprise

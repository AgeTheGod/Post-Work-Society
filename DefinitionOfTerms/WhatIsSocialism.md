#   What Is Socialism?

Socialism is a social and economic theory that advocates for the collective ownership and control of the means of production and distribution of goods and services. 
The idea is that by having the state control these critical services, it can ensure that they are provided to everyone in society, regardless of their ability to pay.

However, (and this is an important point), Socialism does not mandate that all means of production must be provided by the state and private ownership is illegal (I believe that's Communism which is a separate Social Theory) but does mandate that all critical services essential to the well-being of Society should be controlled by the state. 

Beyond those critical services people are then free to produce any "luxury" goods or provide any "community services" that they wish.

That's pretty much the case across the entire planet and really the arguments come down to how much shoudl be provide by the state as "essential services" and how much is left to others, both individuals and cortporations, to provide as "value add services"?

That final question is what distinguishes the many variants of Socialism from each other.

In addition, the motivation from providing a Service under Socialism is much different to Capitalism.

The economic motivation, i.e. the desired outcome that causes the economic activity to occur, in Capitalism is "Profit" whereas the economic motivation in Socialism is "Social Benefit".
In all other respects the Factors of Production remain the same.

Consequently, we can define an Enterprise as being a Socialist Enterprise if its...
- ultimate beneficial owner is Society as a whole
- intent is to provide an essential service to all members of that Society at minimal cost and not for profit.
- production targets are set by Society rather than by the Enterprise

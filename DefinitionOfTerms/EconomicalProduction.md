#   What is Economical Production?

The "_Economics of Production_" is the branch of Economics that deals with the process of combining resource inputs to manufacture goods or services that satisfy human needs and wants.

However, with "**Economical Production**" I'm using a slightly different term because in a Post Work Society the _motivation for production_ completely changes from "_maximising profit_" to "_maximising the public benefit that a Society receives from that production_".

In this context "_Economical_" means using resources carefully to avoid waste, save money, and maximize efficiency. 
It describes choices, products, or behaviors that offer the best value or utility with the least amount of expense or effort.
An item, strategy, or process is considered economical if it meets one or more these criteria:
- Cost-Effective: It costs less upfront or saves money over time compared to alternatives.
- Resource-Efficient: It uses minimal fuel, time, labor, or raw materials to complete a task.
- High-Yield: It delivers maximum functional output or value relative to the inputs used.


We still apply the Economics Of Production to analysing a Production Process for the purpose of reducing (or removing) cost from the Production Process but we are doing that to minimize the cost to the consumer rather than to increase the Profit made by the Enterprise.

##  Factors of Production

The Economics of Production is based on four distinct resource pillars known as the [Factors Of Production](./FactorsOfProduction) summarized as...

| Resource Input    | Purpose                                                                                                                                       |
|-------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Natural Resources | All naturally occurring resources whose supply was inherently provided by nature including Plants and Animals as well as inanimate resources. |
| Labour            | The physical and mental exertion of human beings used to create goods or services.                                                            |
| Tools             | Any previously created man-made artefacts (including Money) used by Labour to convert Natural Resources into more Man-Made Artefacts          |
| Motivation        | The people who identify a demand for some product or service and organize an enterprise to meet that demand.                                  |

Traditionally in both Classical Economics and Neo-Classical Economics, the motivation for Enterprise is "Profit", usually Monetary Profit, where the Enterprise produces goods & services and sells them on an open market with the intent of increasing the wealth of the enterprise and its investors.

In a Post Work Society this motivation completely changes because the motivation to produce is driven by **maximising the public benefit that a Society receives from that production**.

However, irrespective of the Motivation, the Factors of Production do not change and the purpose of applying Economics to the production process do not change, i.e. to improve the efficiency of production, 

## Production Process Frameworks

Production Processes are generally organized using four primary operational structures that in order of increasing complexity and scale are...

1. Job Production: Creating one-off, unique, and fully customized items tailored to single clients.
2. Batch Production: Manufacturing limited quantities of identical products in distinct group runs.
3. Mass Production: Generating high volumes of standardized products using uniform assembly lines.
4. Flow Production: Continuous, uninterrupted material movement through a sequential manufacturing process.

With the Post Work Society and Public Benefit Services we are exclusively looking at the last two frameworks because these are the two frameworks that are most open to efficiency gains when services are continuously provided to a very large number of people.
So whenever I mention a Production Process I am, as a minimum, talking about a Mass Production Process.

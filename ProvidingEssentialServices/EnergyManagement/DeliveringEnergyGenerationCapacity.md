#   Delivering Energy Generation Capacity

Each of the identified Energy Generation Technologies already exists and producing energy somewhere in the world. 
As a result the role that each plays as either a baseload generator and the maintenance profile (how long it lasts and what needs repairing) are already well established

| Energy Source      | CAPEX / kW                  | LCOE per MWh | Build Time | Average Lifespan | Grid Role               | Maintenance Profile                                                         |
|--------------------|-----------------------------|--------------|:-----------|------------------|-------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Geothermal         | High (~$4,000–$6,000)       | $66 – $109   | 5+ years   | 50 - 100 years   | Baseload (24/7)         | High upfront drilling costs, medium maintenance (scaling).                  |
| Solar PV (Utility) | Very Low (~$900–$1,200)     | $24 – $60    | 1+ month   | 25 - 30 years    | Intermittent (Day)      | Extremely fast and cheap to build, very low maintenance.                    |
| Onshore Wind       | Low (~$1,200–$1,500)        | $33          | 6+ months  | 20 - 25 years    | Intermittent            | Highly cost-efficient, but high mechanical maintenance on blades/gearboxes. |
| Hydro-electric     | Very High (~$3,000–$7,000+) | $62          | 5+ years   | 50 - 100+ years  | Baseload / Dispatchable | Massive civil engineering costs, highly reliable with low opex.             |
| Nuclear            | Extreme (~$8,000–$14,000+)  | $140 – $220+ | 10+ years  | 60 - 80 years    | Baseload (24/7)         | Massive regulatory and construction hurdles, highly complex maintenance.    |

However, as regularly noted elsewhere, technology keeps improving over time with new discoveries and innovations continuously making their way into production.
This frequently results in the Capital Expenditure costs or the Levelized Cost of Energy decreasing.
Based on the latest planned technologies these two values are projected to change as follows...

As always we have the balance between [Quick, Good and Cheap](../../Principles/FastGoodCheap.md) to consider where the most predictable energy sources also happen to be the most expensive and generally take longer to build but last longer once built.

However the critical trends are worth pointing out...
- The Baseload Compression: When looking at the rows for Enhanced Geothermal (Next-Gen Horizontal) and Nuclear (Next-Gen SMR), you can see a massive paradigm shift. Instead of waiting up to 14 years for 24/7 carbon-free grid power, utilities can now deploy continuous energy generation solutions in under 5 years.
- EGS Drastically Outpaces Standard Methods: Separating the rows highlights how horizontal oil-rig drilling methods have radically changed the math for Enhanced Geothermal Systems. By dropping the build time down to 2–4 years and shaving nearly $2,000 per kilowatt off construction costs, next-gen EGS is rapidly becoming competitive with conventional geothermal.
- Solar's Total Economic Advantage: Next-generation Perovskite solar cells cement the technology as the fastest and cheapest option to deploy on the modern grid, with build times dropping to under 9 months and generation costs trending under $35 per Megawatt-hour.
- The SMR & EGS Race: Advanced technologies are completely redefining the baseline timeline for 24/7 clean energy. Historically, a utility company had to wait a decade for clean baseload power from standard Nuclear or Hydro. Next-gen EGS and SMR nuclear can now match that 24/7 output profile while being completed in less than 4 years, dramatically lowering the financial risk of long-term capital investments.
 
However, although the above reflects the physical construction phase from breaking ground to commercial operation in many countries there is also the time required to gain the relevant environmental permits and frid connections before physical construction is even legally permitted to begin.
This is especially the case for Wind, Geothermal, and Nuclear and can add up to 5 years to the overall time-scales.

I'm not proposing that we remove the need to gain the necessary permissions but the process could be streamlined.

In particular we should proactively apply [Predictive Analytics](../../EnablingTechnologies/ComputerizedSystems) to predictive future energy requirements well in advance of the need materializing.

We should also be shifting generation capabilities designed to meet variable consumption requirements closer to the consumer and hence removing it from the grid.
This would all be part of building [Smart Houses](../Housing/SmartHouses).

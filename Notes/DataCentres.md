
    Prompt: What are the current plans for Data Centres in Space?

Data centres in space have rapidly shifted from science fiction to active commercial planning, driven by the AI-induced energy and land crisis on Earth. Tech giants, defense contractors, and specialized startups are actively filing regulatory plans and launching prototype hardware to move high-density artificial intelligence (AI) workloads into Low Earth Orbit (LEO) and onto the lunar surface. [1, 2, 3, 4]

## Major Industry Plans & Players## SpaceX (AI1 Compute Satellites)

*
* Scale: Submitted an application to the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) for a mega-constellation of up to one million data centre satellites. [3, 5]
* Hardware: Developing the AI1 Compute Satellite, which scales up the Starlink platform to include heavy computing payloads. Each satellite will draw 120–150 kW of consistent solar power and launch via Starship. [6, 7, 8]
* Supply Chain: Partnering with Tesla and Intel to build Terafab, a $119 billion, 10-million-square-foot semiconductor facility in Austin, Texas, to supply the massive volume of chips needed. [5]
*

## Alphabet / Google (Project Suncatcher) [3]

*
* Focus: Exploring orbital AI model training using radiation-hardened Tensor Processing Units (TPUs).
* Orbit: Constellations will operate in dawn-dusk sun-synchronous LEO to maintain near-constant exposure to sunlight.
* Timeline: Collaborating with Planet Labs to launch initial test constellations and two prototype satellites by early 2027. [2, 3, 5, 6, 9]
*

## Blue Origin (Project Sunrise)

*
* Scale: Filed FCC plans for 51,600 data centre satellites under its "Project Sunrise" initiative.
* Timeline: Deployment of its TeraWave satellite constellation is scheduled to begin in the fourth quarter of 2027. [5]
*

## Specialized Startups & Other Nations

*
* Starcloud: Deployed a test satellite fitted with an Nvidia H100 GPU to validate advanced AI workloads in space, with goals to reach terrestrial-scale data capacity by 2030.
* NVIDIA: Unveiled the Space-1 Vera Rubin Module, a dedicated, space-rated GPU computing platform.
* Axiom Space: Secured funding from the Texas Space Commission to build its Orbital Data Center (ODC) architecture, targeted heavily at U.S. missile defense and military applications.
* Lonestar Data Holdings: Looking beyond orbit to the Moon for ultra-secure "deep-vault" data storage. They successfully verified a book-sized data centre on the lunar surface via the Intuitive Machines Athena lander.
* China: Already operationalized the Three-Body Computing Constellation (via Adaspace), running Alibaba's Qwen3 AI model directly in orbit. [3, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14]

## The Economic & Technical Drivers
Terrestrial data centres are facing extreme permitting delays and power grid constraints, sometimes taking 5 to 7 years to secure grid connections. Space offers distinct core advantages: [15, 16]

*
* Abundant Power: Continuous, predictable solar radiation in orbit eliminates the need for complex grid interconnections, regional permitting, or terrestrial carbon footprints. [17]
* Edge Processing: Modern Earth Observation (EO) satellites capture terabytes of data daily. Processing data directly in orbit allows companies to beam back only the compressed analysis results, drastically saving downlink bandwidth. [17, 18]
* Security & Continuity: Moving critical sovereign data, defense intelligence, and archival data off-planet isolates it from terrestrial natural disasters, geopolitical infrastructure attacks, and physical hacking. [13, 19]
*

------------------------------
## Technical Bottlenecks
While the economic feasibility models predict parity with Earth by the mid-2030s as launch costs fall below $200/kg, critical engineering hurdles remain: [5, 20, 21, 22, 23]

*
* The Cooling Paradox: While space is inherently cold, it is a vacuum. Without air, heat cannot be convected away from servers. Instead, systems must rely entirely on massive passive infrared radiators, requiring square kilometres of surface area for large deployments.
* Inter-Satellite Latency: AI workloads require massive data exchange between chips. Clustered satellite formations must maintain ultra-compact positions while executing high-bandwidth, free-space optical (laser) links to sync data between units.
* Debris & Degradation: Millions of pieces of orbital debris pose a constant collision risk to wide-area solar panels and radiators. Furthermore, standard chips degrade rapidly under cosmic radiation, necessitating specialized, radiation-tolerant hardware. [9, 17, 24, 25, 26, 27]

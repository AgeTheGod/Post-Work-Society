    prompt: How far from the sun before solar power stops being viable for generating electricity?

Solar power stops being practically viable for generating electricity at a distance of 5 to 10 Astronomical Units (AU) from the sun, which equates to roughly 465 million to 930 million miles (750 million to 1.5 billion kilometres). This range places the absolute limit of modern solar utility between the orbits of Jupiter and Saturn. [1, 2]
## The Physics: The Inverse-Square Law
Sunlight intensity does not drop off in a straight, linear line. Instead, it follows the inverse-square law. If you double your distance from the sun, you receive only one-quarter ($\frac{1}{2^2}$) of the light energy. [3, 4]

* At Earth (1 AU): Sunlight delivers roughly 1,360 watts per square metre (W/m²).
* At Mars (~1.5 AU): Sunlight drops to roughly 600 W/m² (about 44% of Earth's intensity).
* At Jupiter (~5.2 AU): Sunlight plummets to just 50–55 W/m² (under 4% of Earth's intensity).
* At Saturn (~9.5 AU): Sunlight drops to a meager 15 W/m² (barely 1% of Earth's intensity). [3, 4, 5, 6, 7]

## Practical Boundaries in Space Exploration

* The Current Practical Limit (Jupiter): NASA's [Juno spacecraft](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/juno/) and ESA's [JUICE probe](https://www.reddit.com/r/askscience/comments/2mb7qr/how_far_out_into_space_can_sunlight_travel_where/) successfully use solar power at Jupiter. However, to generate a modest 500 watts of electricity (barely enough to power a kitchen blender or refrigerator), Juno requires three massive solar arrays spanning 20 metres (65 feet) across. If those same panels were near Earth, they would generate a massive 14,000 watts. [8, 9, 10, 11, 12]
* The Engineering Breakthrough (The Trojan Asteroids): NASA's Lucy mission pushes solar power slightly further out to 5.7 AU (530 million miles) to study Jupiter's Trojan asteroids. It relies on two colossal, circular solar arrays stretching 7.3 metres (24 feet) in diameter just to keep its systems alive. [13]
* The Hard Ceiling (Saturn and Beyond): Beyond 5 to 6 AU, solar arrays become entirely too heavy, massive, and expensive to launch relative to the tiny amount of electricity they produce. At Saturn's distance and beyond, space agencies abandon solar energy entirely and rely on Nuclear Power—specifically [Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generators (RTGs)](https://www.quora.com/How-far-can-solar-energy-travel) powered by plutonium-238. [1, 8, 14, 15]
